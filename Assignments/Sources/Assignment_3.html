1. Problem, user, do-nothing

Karim, Tier-1 payment Ops specialist, investigates disconnected records before replying. P2 provides 800 merchants, 12,000 tickets/month and 40% investigation time; S1 assumes 18-minute handling time, including 7.2 minutes investigation. Do nothing: manual tools/macros and 4% annual efficiency gain (€57,600), versus a €125,000 alternative project. No actual internal logs or time study are available here.

2. The process — as-is and to-be

Six human steps: read 1.5→1.0 min; search 5.7→1.5; draft 4.8→0.8; check 2.5→1.3; review/send 2.0→2.0; log 1.5→1.0. Before 18.0, after 7.6 min, a 57.8% hypothesis on eligible tracing, not a result. The two pain steps are search and drafting. Step-level error rates, cost, source types, failed automation tests and metrics: annex §2.

L1 entity suggestions → L3 tenant-scoped candidate retrieval → human match confirmation → L2 cited draft and checks → person-only send; bounded audit metadata L4. At any step, manual handling or escalation preserves safe evidence and reason. Failed identity exposes no transaction data. The governance line is verified tenant/transaction before drafting, then human approval before communication.

3. Features — RICE and MoSCoW

MUST (3): tenant/policy gate with handover; cited tracing timeline/draft; paired timing and grading. SHOULD: citation-preview polish. COULD: feedback shortcut. WON'T (5): automatic CRM tags, autonomous replies, dispute deadlines, KYC/AML explanations, refunds/settlement execution. Eight scored candidates, sorted RICE. Reach: 6,000 tracing/month ×75% eligible =4,500, from S2.

Drafting plus timing tests the flip assumption, so both are MUST despite lower RICE. The hardest cut is dispute drafting: attractive value, costly deadline errors and no direct test of the dominant assumption.

4. Data

20 self-contained synthetic cases; AI-proposed labels await human approval. Future sources: authenticated merchant session, read-only ledger/clearing events, versioned policy. FR/EN/DE, EUR SEPA and USD SWIFT initially; rights, retention, isolation and gaps in governance S3.

5. Model — one rung per part

Rules for permissions, money, freshness and audit; pretrained structured extraction because no labelled training corpus exists; hybrid retrieval/drafting because a standalone pretrained model has no current ledger facts; exact checks plus model critique because an LLM judge cannot enforce identity. Component choices and lower-rung alternatives; test rule templates first in S4.

6. Where it lives

Zendesk sidebar: verified merchant, source timestamps, editable cited draft, all-source inspection, explicit review and timestamped specialist handover. Manual mode disables draft insertion; compliance routes to its specialist. Targets: retrieval p95 ≤2s, complete draft ≤10s; timeout suppresses drafting. Logs restricted to assigned agent and authorized Ops/security reviewers. Local clickable prototype, no live connections.

7. Success criterion

By 8 November 2026, on a frozen 200-case synthetic tracing benchmark with eligibility labelled before execution and paired human timing, FlowAssist saves at least 41.2% of total active handling time on eligible cases versus manual handling, including review and fallback time, with QA ≥92%, grounding-check precision ≥98%, and zero wrong-transaction disclosures, cross-tenant exposures, unauthorized sends or monetary actions.

S3 supplies 20 seeds, not measured success. Denominator and grading. A tracing-only pass does not validate S2's dispute/compliance value or authorize production.

8. Risks and rollback trigger

Wrong match, stale facts, fabricated certainty, injection and review fatigue. Any critical exposure or attempted send/fund write immediately disables drafting; Ops owns the stop, Engineering enforces it, DPO/CCO assesses the incident. Failed performance means redesign, not a lowered threshold.

9. What the users changed

No user-driven change is claimed. The v2 strengthens source inspection (D01), explains the missing reference directly (D02), preserves snapshot timestamps (D03), names the specialist destination (D04), and disables draft insertion in manual mode (D05). These are design decisions derived from the fixtures and safety contract, documented in the decision register, without interviews or participant tests.

10. What users could not decide

CFO/Product owns the 41.2% hurdle; timing and independent graders establish performance; Engineering selects the model after evaluation; DPO/Legal confirms lawful use and retention; CCO/Ops approves scope. Users inform workflow and wording.

Spec skeleton and measurement plan

User, trigger and scope

Karim receives an assigned merchant ticket. Confirm identity and intent; retrieve authorised read-only ledger/clearing evidence; confirm a unique transaction; draft only eligible tracing; inspect all sources; human reviews/edits/sends. Always allow manual handling or safe specialist handover. Disputes, KYC/AML, refunds and autonomous sending remain out of scope.

Input / output contract

Input: ticket ID/text/language, authenticated merchant and assigned specialist, source IDs/timestamps, transaction reference, amount in integer minor units, currency and policy version. Extracted entities are untrusted hints; no model SQL. Output: draft_ready | context_only | human_only, cited draft or null, authorised evidence, block reason and specialist route. No customer-send or financial-write tool exists.

Deterministic gates and state

Verify assigned-agent access and tenant before retrieval. Unique reference, currency, amount/gross-net reconciliation and source identity must agree; amount alone never authorises. Missing/stale/contradictory evidence, malformed output, timeout or sensitive intent suppresses drafts. Provisional freshness ≤5 min, retrieval ≤2s and total pipeline ≤10s require validation. A model score cannot override rules.

loading → draft_ready | context_only | human_only. Insertion requires all sources inspected and explicit approval. Edits invalidate approval; ticket changes reset inspection/review. Manual mode disables insertion; resuming requires fresh review. Handover preserves authorised IDs, timestamps, reason and notes; compliance goes to Tier-2 compliance, tracing to Tier-2 payments. Session restoration never restores approvals. Local receipts and audit are editable browser state, not authenticated production records.

Edge cases and fallback

No/multiple match → request unique reference; foreign/forged tenant → empty context and block; unexplained currency/gross-net mismatch → context only; stale/conflicting feed or timeout → suppress; unsupported language, dispute, fraud or KYC → person; injection cannot change scope; credentials/IBAN are redacted, failed redaction routes manually. Never turn “sent to bank” into “beneficiary credited” or invent an ETA, deadline, refund or hold-release advice.

Done criterion and denominator

Use the one-sentence success criterion in brief v2: frozen 200 synthetic tracing cases, eligibility labelled before execution, ≥41.2% total active-time saving including review and fallback; QA ≥92%, unsafe-draft flag precision ≥98%, zero critical exposures. Saving = 1 − sum(assisted active time) / sum(manual active time) over all pre-labelled eligible cases; failures stay in the denominator. Randomise/counterbalance paired timing; report raw intervals, agent count and uncertainty. Do not replace measured manual time by the 18-minute assumption.

Two independent graders check tenant/transaction, money/status/time, citations, safe action and wording. QA rubric: factual accuracy 40, source coverage 25, policy safety 25, clarity 10; critical failure overrides score. Grounding precision = unsafe flags confirmed unsafe / all unsafe flags; also report recall and false-safe count. No flags means undefined precision. Disagreement remains fail/pending adjudication.

Seed origin and freeze

The 20 inputs and initial labels are AI-generated and not human-approved. Two real reviewers must independently check every label/reference and reconcile before freezing a commit and policy version. Keep development/demo cases separate from held-out evaluation, deduplicate by scenario family. Expand to 50 for A4, then 200 tracing cases. The seed does not prove performance or represent the production mix. Seven cases are proposed timing-eligible; thirteen exercise blocking/handover.

Customer isolation and known gaps

Server-side assigned-agent authorization → tenant-constrained parameterized query → authorized source set → redaction → model prompt. Recheck tenant and transaction identity on citations and draft insertion. Index namespace and any cache key include merchant_id; no global historical-ticket search. Failed auth returns empty context and an access-denied state. A model “ignore tenant” instruction can never change scope. Test unauthorized references, mixed-tenant results, stale cached responses and log access before enabling live use.

Gaps: tiny synthetic sample; no real distributions, accented German/colloquial French review, non-EU rails, unsupported languages, unusual currency precision, complex multi-party payments or live permission tests. PT goes to a person; dispute/fraud/KYC goes to specialists in every language. Report failures and fallbacks by language, plan, rail, region and severity; do not claim fairness or accuracy from one or two cases.
